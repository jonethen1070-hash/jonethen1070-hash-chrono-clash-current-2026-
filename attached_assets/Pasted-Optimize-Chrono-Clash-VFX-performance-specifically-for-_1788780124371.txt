Optimize Chrono Clash VFX performance specifically for rapid gameplay, cascades, and multiple simultaneous matches.

IMPORTANT:
Do NOT reduce the game’s responsiveness or slow down the swap/fall/match animations to solve performance problems.

The goal is:
FAST GAMEPLAY + SATISFYING VFX + STABLE PERFORMANCE.

FIRST:
Inspect the existing VFX, particle, glow, animation/tween, DOM/canvas elements, CSS filters, shadows, and effect cleanup systems before changing anything.

Do NOT create a second competing VFX system.
Improve the existing implementation.

1. IDENTIFY VFX PERFORMANCE HOTSPOTS
    Look for:

* Particle spawning during matches
* Repeated particle creation/destruction
* Excessive DOM elements
* CSS blur/filter effects
* Large box-shadow/glow effects
* Multiple simultaneous tweens
* Screen shake
* Flash effects
* Match trails
* Gem glow effects
* Cascade effects
* Power-up VFX
* Effects that remain alive after they visually finish
* requestAnimationFrame loops that continue unnecessarily
* setTimeout/setInterval chains that accumulate during fast play

Fix actual bottlenecks instead of guessing.

2. EFFECT POOLING
    Where practical, reuse VFX objects/elements instead of constantly creating new ones.

For frequently triggered effects such as:

* gem match pop
* particles
* sparkle bursts
* small impact effects
* landing effects

Use lightweight pooling/reuse.

When an effect finishes:

* reset it
* hide it
* return it to the pool
* do not leave inactive animation loops running

3. LIMIT SIMULTANEOUS VFX
    Add a sensible maximum number of simultaneous cosmetic effects.

During normal gameplay:

* allow the full premium VFX treatment.

During large cascades:

* automatically reduce duplicate/similar cosmetic effects.

For example:
If 10+ gems match at once, do NOT spawn 10 expensive particle systems with dozens of particles each.

Instead:

* preserve the strongest match effects
* combine or simplify secondary effects
* maintain visual richness without excessive GPU/CPU work

The player should still SEE and FEEL the cascade.

4. ADAPTIVE VFX QUALITY
    Create a lightweight adaptive VFX quality mechanism based on current effect load.

Normal state:
FULL VFX.

Heavy cascade:
REDUCED VFX.

Very heavy effect load:
MINIMAL SECONDARY VFX.

Never disable the important gameplay feedback.

Priority order:

1. Gem movement
2. Match recognition
3. Match impact/pop
4. Cascade/falling feedback
5. Power-up feedback
6. Secondary particles
7. Decorative ambient effects

If performance becomes stressed, remove/reduce items from the bottom of this list first.

5. EXPENSIVE CSS EFFECTS
    Inspect existing CSS for expensive effects such as:

* filter: blur()
* backdrop-filter
* large box-shadow
* repeated text-shadow
* excessive opacity layers
* large translucent overlays

Do NOT remove the premium visual style.

Instead:

* reduce expensive effects during heavy gameplay
* use simpler opacity/scale effects where visually equivalent
* avoid stacking many blurred layers at the same time
* restore the full visual treatment once the cascade finishes

6. PARTICLE MANAGEMENT
    Particles should be:

* short-lived
* lightweight
* capped
* efficiently removed

Avoid spawning unnecessary particles for every tiny movement.

Use fewer but better particles.

Match VFX should feel:
CRISP
BRIGHT
IMPACTFUL
SHORT

Not:
HEAVY
SMOKY
SLOW
OVERLOADED

7. GEM FALL / LANDING VFX
    Keep the satisfying physical falling effect from the previous speed pass.

For falling gems:

* prioritize movement and landing
* use a tiny landing squash/settle
* use a very subtle impact sparkle only when useful
* do not create expensive particle effects for every falling gem

For large cascades, consolidate landing effects where possible.

8. SWAP VFX
    Keep the swap fast and satisfying.

Use subtle feedback:

* tiny highlight/energy response
* quick scale/impact
* optional very short trail

Do NOT add heavy particles to every normal swap.

9. MATCH VFX
    The match should remain the visual star.

Use:

* quick gem pop
* short glow burst
* controlled particles
* optional energy streak
* concise impact

Make the effect finish quickly so the next cascade can begin immediately.

10. CASCADE VFX
    Make cascades visually exciting without multiplying the performance cost.

As cascade depth increases:

* preserve the important impact
* reduce repetitive secondary particles
* avoid spawning the same expensive effect hundreds of times

A large cascade should look MORE exciting, not slower.

11. CLEANUP
    Audit every temporary VFX element.

Make sure finished effects:

* stop their animation loop
* stop timers
* release references where appropriate
* return pooled elements
* do not continue consuming CPU/GPU

Pay particular attention to effects triggered repeatedly during rapid swiping.

12. FAST-PLAY TEST
    After implementation, test:

* rapid consecutive swaps
* fast 3-match
* simultaneous matches
* 4/5-gem matches
* large cascades
* multiple cascade chains
* power-up + cascade
* rapid gameplay for at least 30–60 seconds

Watch specifically for:

* FPS drops
* input delay
* delayed gem movement
* animation queues building up
* particles accumulating
* VFX remaining after they should disappear
* increasing memory/CPU usage during long play

13. IMPORTANT GAME-FEEL RULE
    Do NOT solve performance by making:

* swaps slower
* matches slower
* gem falling slower
* cascade delays longer
* input less responsive

Instead, optimize the VFX workload.

FINAL TARGET:

FAST SWIPE
↓
INSTANT SATISFYING SWAP
↓
QUICK MATCH POP
↓
PHYSICAL GEM FALL
↓
SMOOTH CASCADE
↓
EXCITING BUT CONTROLLED VFX

The game should feel energetic even when the player plays extremely fast.

Preserve the existing:

* gameplay rules
* scoring
* board
* gem designs
* HUD
* premium 2.5D visual direction
* existing VFX style

This is a performance + game-feel optimization pass, not a visual redesign.
